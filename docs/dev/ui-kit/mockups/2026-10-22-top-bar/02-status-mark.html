A status mark instead of the green dot

Today's dot says one thing: this page's socket is open. The command line says the same thing again as ONLINE. What an operator wants from the corner is more: am I talking to the station, is the station the master or a member of a LumaSession, how many stations, is it slow or busy, is the output on. The mark answers those in one glance and opens the details on a tap; the bottom ONLINE key can then go.

Three designs, every state

Green = all well; blue = a member following a master; amber = waiting (busy, reconnecting); red = lost; grey = output off. The letter is the station (--station A); a member shows its latency to the master.

Why the ring is recommended

  • It keeps the corner small: the strip (page 1) needs the room for the show and the modes.
  • Its shape changes with the state, not only its colour: full, hollow (output off), half (a member), spinning (busy), blinking (reconnecting). That reads for colour-blind eyes too.
  • The station letter next to it is what you need when two stations sit side by side (A and B on one desk).

The chip says the most in words, but it is the widest and competes with the Show tab. The signal is good at “how fast” but weak at “which role”.

To decide on this page

1. The mark: the ring (recommended), the chip or the signal?
2. Left or right? A1 S1 puts the dot at the far right, after the clock; the ring is drawn at the left on page 1, where the word LumaCore is today.
3. Does the bottom ONLINE key go (its menu moves to the mark's pop-up)?
4. Should the word “LumaCore” stay beside the mark, or only the station letter?
